# Case Selection Rules

## The First Question

Before answering, ask:

> What is this problem most like in the book set?

Not:

> Which book name sounds impressive here?

## Good Match Criteria

A strong match usually has:

1. structural similarity
2. a vivid retellable case
3. a principle that survives transfer
4. writing value after transfer

## Match Levels

### High

- same type of mechanism
- strong explanatory lift
- low distortion risk

### Medium

- partial mechanism overlap
- useful for framing, not full explanation
- must state limits

### Low

- mostly metaphorical
- risk of overreach is high
- use only if clearly labeled as a loose analogy

## Red Flags

Do not force a match when:

- the user's problem is primarily legal, medical, or current-event driven
- the book case only matches at the level of vocabulary
- the principle would hide important context
- the analogy sounds elegant but weakens judgment

## Preferred Mapping Logic

Use:

- `Sapiens` for shared belief, coordination, institutions, brands, identity stories
- `Thinking, Fast and Slow` for bias, judgment, fast intuition, decision mistakes
- `Atomic Habits` for behavior design, repetition, friction, environment
- `Influence` for persuasion triggers and compliance patterns
- `The Black Swan` for uncertainty, fragility, tail risk, hindsight traps
- `Scale` for growth, network effects, large-system constraints
- `Out of Control` for self-organization, ecosystems, emergent order
- `A Brief History of Time` for limits, gravity, lock-in, observer perspective

## Required Sentence When Weak

If the analogy is weak, explicitly say:

`This is a loose analogy, useful for framing, but not a full explanation of the problem.`
